## How accent labels were grouped

Common Voice accent labels are self-reported free text; a speaker can list several, separated by `|`. Each piece is lowercased, has periods removed, and is checked against the rules below in order (first match wins). A clip is kept only if **every informative piece maps to the same group**. Pieces marked IGNORE (e.g. "neutral") are skipped; any EXCLUDE, unmapped, or conflicting piece removes the clip.

### Rules (in order)

| # | Result | Pattern (regex) | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IGNORE | `^(neutral\|standard\|normal\|general\|none\|no accent\|native\|native speaker\|slight\|slight accent\|light\|light accent\|clear)( accent)?$` | Describes accent strength, not which accent |
| 2 | L2_MARKER | `^(non[- ]?native\|foreign\|second language\|esl\|learner)( speaker\| english\| accent)?$` | Says the speaker is non-native but not which first language |
| 3 | EXCLUDE | `\bimpediment\|\bstutter\|\bstammer\|\blisp\b\|\brhotacism\|\bdysarthria\|\bhearing (loss\|impair)\|\bdeaf\b` | Speech or hearing difference, not an accent; would confound the accent comparison |
| 4 | EXCLUDE | `\bsouth atlantic\|\bfalkland\|\bsaint helena\|\bst helena\|\bsouthatlandtic` | South Atlantic: too few speakers for its own group |
| 5 | EXCLUDE | `\bmix\|\bblend\|\bhybrid\|\bbetween\b\|\bcombination\b\|\bmulti` | Self-described mixed accent: can't be placed in one group |

## Sampling

From the eligible pool, up to 400 clips were drawn per group with a speaker-balanced, sentence-first greedy procedure:

1. Each sentence is scored by how many groups have at least one eligible clip of it.
2. Sampling runs in passes with a rising per-speaker cap: pass 1 allows 1 clip per speaker, pass 2 allows 2, up to 10. A group only returns to a speaker once it has run out of new speakers.
3. Within each pass, sentences are visited from most-shared to least-shared (ties in random order, fixed seed); every group that has the sentence and is not yet full takes one clip of it, from the speaker with the fewest clips selected so far.
4. No sentence appears twice within a group. Each group stops at the target.

Speaker spread comes first because clips from the same person are not independent: 400 clips from 400 speakers say far more about a group than 400 clips from 40. Preferring shared sentences then makes groups read the same sentences where possible, so WER differences are less likely to come from some groups getting harder sentences.

## Ethics and known limitations

- Results are reported only at group level. No attempt is made to identify speakers, and Common Voice client IDs are replaced with project-local pseudonyms.
- Accent labels are self-reported and inconsistent; the grouping rules above are a judgement call and different rules could change the results.
- Groups pool together real variation (e.g. all of the US, or all European first languages). A group's WER is an average over that variation.
- Common Voice is read speech recorded on contributors' own devices; recording quality may differ between groups.
- Contributors are not a random sample of each accent's speakers.
